Thailand Rebuilds a 66-Pound Rodent From Two Teeth

What happened

A McGill University research digest published November 10 highlighted Yakamys sirindhornae, a newly described giant rodent from Thailand's Krabi Basin that lived about 34 million years ago. The specimen consists of a lower second molar and an associated incisor. Seven dental equations produced body-mass estimates from 21.4 to 47.3 kilograms; the study's median was 28.6 kilograms and its mean 31.6, supporting a cautious rounded description of roughly 30 kilograms, or 66 pounds.

Why it matters

The animal pushes extreme rodent size deep into the late Eocene or earliest Oligocene, earlier than the better-known giant rodents of South America. But a tooth-based mass estimate is a model, not a fossil weigh-in. The authors explicitly note uncertainty created by the limited material and by applying relationships from living rodents to an extinct lineage. The useful result is a supported size range and an evolutionary signal—not a photograph, exact body plan or known coat color.
