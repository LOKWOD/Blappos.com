California’s Ocean Opens a Tropical Guest List

What happened

Reuters reported October 17 that an unusually strong El Niño, following an extended marine heat wave, has carried an exceptional mix of tropical marine life into Southern California waters. The report documents California’s first recorded longfin bigeye fish, two olive ridley nests that NOAA called the first recorded sea-turtle nests on the U.S. West Coast, a rare yellow-bellied sea snake, and recent whale-shark, hammerhead, flying-fish and manta-ray sightings.

Why it matters

The arrivals are not simply a beachside curiosity. Warm water can move mobile fish and sharks north while stressing cold-water kelp that supports less-mobile species and entire coastal food webs. Experts quoted by Reuters expect conditions to mostly normalize after El Niño subsides next year, but the episode is a useful live demonstration of how a natural climate cycle and a preceding marine heat wave can rapidly rearrange an ecosystem without proving that every individual sighting has one identical cause.
