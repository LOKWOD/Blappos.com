Ancient DNA Announces That a New Cow Just Dropped

What happened

Reuters reported October 19 that researchers recovered 20 low-coverage genomes from tiny bovine bone fragments found in Denisova Cave and nearby Altai sites. The oldest fragments date to roughly 250,000–200,000 years ago and the youngest to about 27,000 years ago. The lineage split from the ancestors of living yaks about 400,000 years ago and appears distinct enough to represent a previously unknown species, provisionally nicknamed ‘Yak X.’ It has not received a formal scientific name because the evidence is genomic rather than a diagnostic skeleton.

Why it matters

The work shows how palaeogenomics can turn otherwise unidentifiable fragments into a long population history. Finding the animal across sites used by Denisovans and later humans adds a possible prey species to the Ice Age ecosystem, but proximity is not direct proof that any particular group hunted these exact animals. The formal-name restraint also matters: a striking genome result does not erase the anatomical standards of taxonomy.
