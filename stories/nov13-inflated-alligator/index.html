Sugar Land Dispatches Animal Control to a Runaway Pool Floatie

What happened

UPI reported on November 13 that Sugar Land Animal Services responded to a resident's call about an alligator that appeared sick. Animal Services Officer Perez kept the investigation appropriately cautious, then found that the convincing shape was a green inflatable alligator pool toy that had drifted away. The department said the caller still did the right thing: keep a safe distance from wildlife that looks sick or injured and call trained responders rather than approaching it.

Why it matters

The joke works because real alligators inhabit Sugar Land's ponds and waterways, while distance, glare and partial views can make quick identification unreliable. The city's official guidance says a sighting alone is not necessarily an emergency, but residents should contact Animal Services when an alligator is in a roadway or a pool, lawn or driveway without direct water access, or when it approaches people or repeatedly follows watercraft. This incident does not show that wildlife calls are frivolous; it shows why safe reporting and expert verification beat a risky close look.
