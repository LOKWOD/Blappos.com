Christmas Island Opens the World's Busiest Crab Overpass

What happened

UPI reported on November 16 that millions of Christmas Island red crabs had begun their annual forest-to-coast migration. Parks Australia says the first wet-season rain normally starts the movement, while the moon and tides determine the spawning date; the 2026 migration was carried in daily livestreams, including one from a purpose-built crab bridge. Roads can close temporarily so the animals can cross safely.

Why it matters

The spectacle is also a life-cycle clock. Adult crabs leave inland burrows, travel toward the sea and time spawning for the receding high tide before dawn during the last-quarter moon. The island is often described as supporting about 100 million red crabs, but that is a broad population estimate—not a count of animals on the November 16 livestream or proof that every crab uses one bridge. The defensible lesson is that simple infrastructure and temporary road controls can protect a mass wildlife movement without turning the animals into a traffic stunt.
