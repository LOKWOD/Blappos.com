Atlanta Baggage Claim Discovers the Brown Goo Was Peanut Butter

What happened

WSB-TV reported on November 18 that passengers from American Airlines Flight 2609, arriving in Atlanta from Miami around 8:30 p.m. Monday, waited more than two hours before dozens of bags appeared smeared with a brown substance. Travelers initially feared it might be waste. American Airlines said an inspection found that liquefied peanut butter had leaked from one checked bag and affected other luggage, apologized and said it was working with customers. Passengers cleaned their bags at the airport, while some questioned whether one container could explain the volume they saw.

Why it matters

The airline's explanation resolves the most alarming identification without resolving every practical question. The reporting reviewed here does not establish the exact number of affected bags, the size or packaging of the peanut-butter container, an independent contamination test, who packed it or the outcome of every damage claim. It does show why a mundane packing failure can become an airport-scale hygiene scare when one leaking bag moves through shared handling equipment.
