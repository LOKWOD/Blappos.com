Sea Pigs Discover Their Skin Has a Majority Shareholder

What happened

A University of Tokyo-led team examined eight deep-sea sea pigs and reported a newly described symbiotic bacterium living beneath their body surface. In body-wall samples from three sea-pig species, one bacterium accounted for more than 70% of bacterial 16S rRNA gene sequences. The proposed Candidatus Abyssoplasma scotoplanesicola has a compact genome of about 649,000 bases, yet more than a quarter is devoted to surface-associated protein groups, including proteins longer than 5,000 amino acids.

Why it matters

The 70% figure describes bacterial marker-gene sequences in sampled body walls; it does not mean the bacterium makes up 70% of the whole animal. Fluorescence imaging located the cells beneath the body surface, while genome reconstruction suggests an unusually host-dependent partnership. Researchers do not yet know what benefit the sea pigs receive, so 'symbiont' describes close association without proving mutual benefit.
