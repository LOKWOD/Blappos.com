Rhode Island Finds Two Iguanas and Misplaces the Explanation

What happened

The Potter League for Animals reported on November 14 that two large iguanas were found within two days at separate locations on Aquidneck Island. One, more than three feet long, had been lingering in Portsmouth and reportedly climbed onto a resident's front porch. A second, smaller iguana was found miles away in Newport—inside a flower pot. Both were safe at the shelter, which opened a five-day stray hold while asking possible owners to come forward.

Why it matters

The coincidence is real; the connection is not established. Potter League staff did not know whether the reptiles had ever been together, how long they had been outside or where they came from. That distinction matters because two similar animals arriving together can tempt a tidy escaped-pair narrative that the evidence does not support. The practical outcome is simpler: identifiable owners get a short reclaim window, and otherwise the shelter can arrange appropriate care rather than leaving large exotic pets exposed outdoors.
