Boston Gives an Insurance Mascot Its Own Opposition Party

What happened

UPI reported October 28 that dozens of people had gathered outside Liberty Mutual’s Boston headquarters on Sunday to protest the company’s recurring ‘Liberty Biberty’ advertising character. Organizer Connor Harvey, 19, said a video promoting the event had drawn about 600,000 views. The protest was peaceful, signs included ‘Liberty from Biberty,’ and one counter-protester arrived dressed as the mascot.

Why it matters

A joke about repetitive advertising became a real public assembly, showing how online attention can turn brand fatigue into an offline participatory event. The turnout documents a protest against a fictional campaign character—not a policy claim about the insurer, a labor dispute or evidence that every participant shared a deeper cause.
