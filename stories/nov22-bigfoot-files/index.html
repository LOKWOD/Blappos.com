The Forest Service Bigfoot File Ends With Plaster Casts and a Hoax Theory

What happened

A November 22 report examined three Bigfoot/Sasquatch records that the U.S. Forest Service posted in its FOIA reading room on October 15. The centerpiece is a 1982 investigation in Oregon's Umatilla National Forest, where investigators photographed tracks and made plaster casts at two sites. One print was described as roughly 15 inches long and 7 inches wide. Wildlife biologist Rodney Johnson concluded that the impressions were not made by a large two-legged animal; Border Patrol tracker Joel Hardin separately called them a clever hoax and suggested a fabricated foot may have made them.

Why it matters

The federal records are real, but they document reported tracks and the investigation—not a confirmed creature. The file preserves competing field observations, measurements and explicit skepticism. That makes it useful as a public-records lesson: an agency keeping evidence is not the same as the agency endorsing the claim, and a newly posted file can describe a decades-old event.
