Wisconsin Turkey Enters Its Smoke Phase

What happened

UPI reported on November 12 that a Snapshot Wisconsin trail camera photographed an unusually pale wild turkey in Richland County. The Wisconsin Department of Natural Resources identified the bird as showing the smoke color phase: reduced brown and red pigment produces mostly white or light-gray plumage with darker gray-to-black markings, especially on the lower feathers. The agency says the appearance comes from a genetic mutation and is a natural color variation in wild turkeys.

Why it matters

The label explains plumage, not ancestry or health. A smoke-phase bird is not automatically an escaped domestic turkey, an albino or a hybrid, and one camera image cannot establish how common the mutation is across the state. Wisconsin DNR also distinguishes other rare phases, including erythristic birds with excess rusty-red pheomelanin and melanistic birds with excess black eumelanin. The useful lesson is that striking color can be real biology without supporting a broader population claim.
