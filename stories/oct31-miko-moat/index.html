A 1,500-Year-Old Shrine Maiden Waits in the Moat

What happened

Live Science reported October 31 that archaeologists at the Nishikaminomiya site in Gunma Prefecture recovered a roughly 40-centimeter haniwa clay figure from the inner slope of a fifth- or sixth-century burial-mound moat. The official Gunma archaeology notice identifies the figure as a miko, or shrine maiden, and records its shoulder sash; the reported topknot, bracelets, red pigment and ritual rod support the interpretation of a ceremonial figure associated with Kofun-period funerary practice.

Why it matters

The find preserves unusually detailed evidence about ritual clothing and representation in early anthropomorphic haniwa, and the official excavation notice gives readers a durable primary record rather than a headline alone. “Miko” is the archaeologists’ interpretation of iconography, not a surviving name, biography or proof that the figure performed a supernatural act.
