Physicists Ask One Atomic Clock to Keep Two Kinds of Time

What happened

A November 21 ScienceDaily digest highlighted a Kyushu University-led theoretical proposal published earlier in 2026 in Physical Review Letters. The team modeled a trapped-ion optical clock whose quantum motion is placed in a superposition. Because relative motion changes elapsed proper time, the clock's motion can become entangled with its internal energy; the researchers say that loss of quantum coherence could provide a measurable signature. Their control technique is projected to improve sensitivity to the effect by roughly 100 to 1,000 times.

Why it matters

No clock has yet been observed living through two rates of time in this experiment. The result is a proposed way to make a very small quantum-relativistic effect detectable with advanced atomic-clock technology. That distinction matters: a theoretical signal and an experimental platform are a test plan, not a successful measurement or evidence of literal time travel. The researchers say the next step is a detailed real-world experiment that accounts for noise and uncertainty.
