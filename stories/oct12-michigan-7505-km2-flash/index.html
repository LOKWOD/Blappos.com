Michigan Gets a 7,505-Square-Kilometer Flash

What happened

What Weird Shit Happened Today computed from NOAA GOES Geostationary Lightning Mapper data that a flash with a 7,505-square-kilometer optical footprint was detected at 46.89°N, 88.65°W in Michigan at 9:35 p.m. ET on October 12. The source describes the value as a computed observation from NOAA GOES GLM L2 LCFA data, not an official world record.

Why it matters

The Geostationary Lightning Mapper detects brief optical signals from lightning across wide areas, so an optical footprint is a satellite-derived measurement rather than the path of one cartoon bolt on the ground. Most flashes span roughly 10–100 square kilometers, while megaflashes can exceed 1,000; the 7,505-square-kilometer value is extraordinary but should retain its computed-data label.
