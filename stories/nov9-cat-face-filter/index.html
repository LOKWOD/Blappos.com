A Man Sees His Cat's Eyes in Everyone Else's Face

What happened

Dartmouth College reported on November 9 the case of a 28-year-old man identified as Karl who, after removal of part of his right temporal lobe for an arteriovenous malformation, sometimes sees remembered facial features transfer between his gray tabby cat and people. If the cat is the first face he sees, later human faces can appear with almond-shaped eye sockets, enlarged pupils or tabby-like markings; on other days, features from the first human face can appear in later faces and even on the cat.

Why it matters

The Cortex case report describes palinopsia—an earlier visual experience persisting or reappearing—producing a form of prosopometamorphopsia, or distorted face perception. It is one documented neurological case, not evidence that ordinary familiarity with a pet changes everyone's vision, and the authors do not present a general prevalence estimate or treatment. The team worked with Karl to make AI-assisted visualizations, showing how patient-guided images can help clinicians study a subjective perceptual symptom without treating it as a psychiatric delusion.
