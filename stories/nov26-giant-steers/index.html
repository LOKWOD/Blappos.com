Australia Finds Two Steers the Size of Small Cars Because Nobody Stopped Them Growing

What happened

The Guardian reported on November 26 that two Holstein Friesian steers on opposite sides of Australia each stand about 192 centimeters, or 6 feet 4 inches, at the shoulder. Frank has lived for six years with the Companion Cows rescue in Tasmania after leaving a dairy farm. Tiny Tim, an eight-year-old steer in Waroona, Western Australia, weighs about 1,400 kilograms and was kept as a pet after he became too large for the abattoir. Both were measured with a horse measuring stick.

Why it matters

Their height is remarkable but the national superlative remains provisional. The Guardian calls them perhaps the largest cattle in Australia, while ABC notes that Guinness lists the Canadian steer Beef at just under 196 centimeters as the tallest living steer. Frank and Tiny Tim have not displaced that verified world record, and the published reports do not establish an independently adjudicated Australian record. What they do document is how unusually long-lived dairy-breed steers can keep growing far beyond ordinary commercial timelines.
