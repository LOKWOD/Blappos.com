Kansas City Opens a Manhole and Finds a Dog Eight Feet Down

What happened

KCTV reported on November 19 that Kansas City, Kansas, Environmental Services workers doing routine sewer maintenance noticed a displaced manhole cover in a vacant lot near 17th Street and Stewart Avenue. They found a dog at the bottom of the roughly eight-foot-deep opening, called supervisors and Animal Services, and used specialized sewer tools to bring her safely to the surface. Animal Services took the dog for a checkup, and the damaged opening was fenced pending repair.

Why it matters

The rescue began with workers noticing infrastructure out of place, then escalating instead of attempting an improvised solo retrieval. The public account establishes the depth, team response, equipment, transfer for a checkup and temporary barrier; it does not establish how the dog fell, how long she was below, her ownership or a final medical outcome. The damaged cover is both the story's absurd entrance and an ordinary public-safety hazard.
