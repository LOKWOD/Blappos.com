Tiny Viruses Put Evolution's Shell Game Back on the Table

What happened

A University of Miami report highlighted by ScienceDaily on November 23 described a group of small viruses identified by searching environmental databases containing billions of DNA sequences. The Nature Communications study found viruses with HK97-fold shell proteins and unusually small, simple genomes. Their proteins resemble both viral capsids and encapsulins—the protein compartments that cells use to enclose biochemical reactions—and the genomes lack the portal and tail genes found in more complex relatives.

Why it matters

The mixed evolutionary groups and structural similarities support the authors' model that viruses and cellular compartments may have evolved from one another repeatedly through an immature shell state called a procapsid. That is a model built from metagenomic, phylogenetic and predicted-structure evidence, not a time-lapse of early life. Most of the viruses were not cultured, their hosts are unknown and the paper does not show that they cause human disease.
