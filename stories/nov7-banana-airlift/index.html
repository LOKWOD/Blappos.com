Florida Sends a Banana by Air Because the Golf Cart Is Underwater

What happened

The Associated Press reported on November 7 that flooding at Save the Chimps near Fort Pierce submerged internal roads normally used to move about 3,000 pounds of food per day by golf cart. Care houses remained safe on high ground, but a 38-year-old chimpanzee named Kaleb was temporarily cut off in an outdoor area. Staff used an emergency delivery drone to bring him food and blankets until he moved to a more accessible location.

Why it matters

The surreal delivery documents practical redundancy during a real emergency. Some veterinary and other staff stayed overnight, a swamp buggy helped and then broke down, and crews used trucks and a tractor for the rest of the sanctuary's 200 chimpanzees. AP reported that the animals stayed safe, meals remained on schedule and water was receding by Saturday, while flooding still complicated staff access and damaged equipment. The episode is not evidence that consumer drones can replace trained disaster planning or animal-care teams.
