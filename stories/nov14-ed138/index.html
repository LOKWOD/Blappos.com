The Solar System Finds a Dwarf-Planet Candidate in Its Junk Drawer

What happened

A ScienceDaily digest published November 14 highlighted 2026 ED138, a large trans-Neptunian object discovered in early Vera C. Rubin Observatory data. The discovery paper places it about 46 astronomical units from the Sun and estimates a diameter of roughly 612 to 1,530 kilometers depending on how reflective its surface is. Rubin recorded it 30 times during 2026, and archival Dark Energy Camera images extend the observed orbit back to 2016.

Why it matters

ED138 is a strong dwarf-planet candidate, not a sixth officially recognized dwarf planet. Brightness and assumed reflectivity give a wide size range, while follow-up observations are still needed to measure its dimensions, shape and surface. Its late discovery is scientifically useful: the object sits near the crowded Milky Way plane, where earlier searches struggled to separate faint motion from dense background stars. Rubin's repeated wide-field imaging could reveal more large objects in similarly neglected sky regions.
