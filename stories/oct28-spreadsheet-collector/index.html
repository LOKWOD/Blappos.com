A Spreadsheet Collector Passes 1,000 Programs; the Record Still Says 506

What happened

Guinness World Records reported October 28 that Buenos Aires financial adviser Ariel Fischman says his physical spreadsheet-software collection has grown beyond 1,000 items. Guinness certified the collection at 506 unique items in 2018, so 506 remains the official measured record in the published account. The archive began with Fischman’s interest in the history of electronic accounting and includes early software, manuals and multiple editions; the Guinness article highlights VisiCalc, first released in 1978, as a landmark in the category.

Why it matters

A physical software archive preserves packaging, documentation and edition history that disappear when programs become downloads or subscriptions. The two numbers matter: more than 1,000 is the collector’s current total, while 506 is the certified count. Keeping them separate prevents a fresh personal estimate from being silently upgraded into a new verified world record.

The Blappos angle

The October 28 article, 2018 certification, current self-reported total and record boundary end above. The Blappos interpretation: after collecting a thousand spreadsheet programs, the only empty cells left are weekends.
