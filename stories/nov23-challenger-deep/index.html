The Deepest Known Pyroclastic Rocks Turn Up Where Volcanoes Should Behave

What happened

Frontiers reported on November 23 that the ROV Jason/Medea recovered welded pyroclastic rocks at depths greater than 6,000 meters on the forearc near Challenger Deep. The peer-reviewed Frontiers in Earth Science paper identifies them as the deepest known pyroclastic deposits in the ocean. The 2024 expedition reached 6,148 meters at the key site and found evidence of volcanic flows that stayed hot enough to weld after moving downslope, even though the team has not located the eruption vents.

Why it matters

At more than 600 bars of water pressure, gases cannot expand the way they do in an ordinary surface eruption, and volcanism is also unusual this close to a trench. The authors propose that volatile-rich magma and supercritical fluids may explain a distinct, less violent kind of deep-sea explosive volcanism. The rocks are direct evidence; the eruption mechanism, exact vents and detailed geochemistry remain under study.
