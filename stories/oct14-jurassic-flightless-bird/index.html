One of the Oldest Birds Skipped the Flying Part

What happened

A Nature paper published October 14 describes Yuezhuornis yangyuanensis from a single partial skeleton in Fujian’s Late Jurassic Nanyuan Formation, dated to roughly 150–148 million years ago. The crow-sized animal had unusually reduced forelimbs, a long bony tail, serrated teeth and a hyperextensible second toe. Its forelimbs were about one-third shorter than its hindlimbs, well outside the proportions of the 377 living bird species used for comparison.

Why it matters

The authors interpret the anatomy as evidence of a terrestrial, secondarily flightless lifestyle, pushing specialized flightlessness much closer to the origin of birds than earlier examples. That conclusion changes the ecological picture of the earliest avialans, but one incomplete specimen cannot reveal its colors, behavior or exact running speed; those parts of the illustration remain imaginative reconstruction.
