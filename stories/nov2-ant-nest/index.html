England’s Rarest Ant Nest Vanishes by the Spadeful

What happened

UPI reported on November 2 that an entire narrow-headed-ant nest had been removed from Devon Wildlife Trust’s Chudleigh Knighton Heath reserve. The trust’s same-day primary account says researchers discovered a hollow in mid-October where a known nest had been, with spade marks around it. The reserve is the species’ last English stronghold; its latest survey counted 142 active nests. A mature colony can hold roughly 1,000 to 5,000 ants and one or more queens that may live for as long as 25 years.

Why it matters

The 1,000–5,000 figure describes the normal range for a nest, not a counted inventory of the missing colony. The serious part is replacement time: a mature colony represents years of slow growth in a nationally rare species, so losing one nest is not equivalent to losing one season’s insects. Devon and Cornwall Police are treating the reserve damage as a reported crime; the published evidence does not identify a suspect or motive.
