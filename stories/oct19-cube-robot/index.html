Four Engineering Students Reduce a Cube to 9.806 Seconds

What happened

UPI reported October 19 that University of Canterbury students Caleb Everitt, Johann Torres, Hayden Watson and Maz Cameron built a robot that solved a scrambled 4-by-4 cube in 9.806 seconds during an official Guinness World Records attempt on October 16. The system used four cameras, an open-source solving algorithm, faster drone motors and suction-cup grippers. The university says Guinness confirmation is still pending.

Why it matters

The current published Guinness record is 45.30 seconds, so the attempt would be a substantial improvement if certified. More important than the stopwatch is the integration: mechanical design, power electronics, computer vision and software all had to agree on the same fraction of a second. Until Guinness completes its review, 9.806 seconds is a documented attempt result—not a certified world record.
