The Amazon Releases a Three-Centimeter Fish in Three Tiny Pools

What happened

A ScienceDaily digest published November 8 highlighted Laimosemion laranja, a newly described killifish from the lower Rio Cajari drainage in Brazil's Amapá state. The peer-reviewed Zoosystematics and Evolution paper identifies the species with morphological characters and mitochondrial DNA; males carry vivid orange spots and chevrons, and the standard body length is under three centimeters. Researchers have found it in only three small forest pools across an estimated 25-square-kilometer range.

Why it matters

A new species can be scientifically valid and already vulnerable at the moment it receives a name. The authors say every known site shows disturbance from deforestation, mining, urban expansion or plastic pollution and argue that the fish meets IUCN criteria for Endangered status. That is a research-team recommendation, not a completed IUCN listing, and three known pools are the current evidence boundary—not proof that no undiscovered population exists elsewhere.
