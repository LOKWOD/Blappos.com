Rome fines golf-cart operators as unauthorized fleet crowds ancient streets

What happened

The Guardian reported October 3 that Rome police intensified checks on golf-cart tours after Adnkronos reported that 90% of roughly 500 carts in circulation lacked authorization. Police stopped 131 carts near major landmarks and issued €38,000 in fines for violations including unlicensed driving and illegal parking. Legitimate operators said the carts help families and visitors with mobility needs.

Why it matters

The dispute joins overtourism, accessibility, street safety and licensing in a city whose historic center already has limited space. The 90% estimate came through a news-agency report, while the stop and fine totals describe recent enforcement—not a finding that every cart or passenger broke rules.

The Blappos angle

Rome in Four-Wheel Drive. Two thousand years after Julius Caesar tried traffic restrictions, the city has rediscovered congestion at golf-course speed. The joke targets the streets-and-tourism situation, not individual visitors or lawful operators.
