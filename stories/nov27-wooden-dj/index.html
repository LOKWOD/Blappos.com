Nairobi DJ Builds a Wooden Deck and Uses a Dinner Fork to Scratch

What happened

A November 27 Africanews report carrying Associated Press reporting profiled 29-year-old Paul Mwangi, who performs in Nairobi as Deejay Boboss on a homemade wooden DJ deck. The working console combines plywood, discarded radio motherboards, bottle-cap controls and towel-holder rails. Mwangi uses an ordinary fork to interrupt contacts on the exposed circuitry and produce scratch-like effects. The project began with a plastic jerrycan converted into a speaker before he expanded the system.

Why it matters

The deck is a vivid example of repair culture, reuse and self-taught electronics turning scarce equipment into a public performance tool. The report verifies that Mwangi built and performs with the rig; it does not publish a replicable circuit design, establish electrical safety, or show that a fork is suitable for ordinary powered electronics. The article's function claim is therefore narrower than a how-to guide.
