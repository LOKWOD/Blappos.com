Arkansas Prints Moon Dust Into Bricks Before the Moon Has Building Codes

What happened

ScienceDaily published a November 20 digest of University of Arkansas research demonstrating small interlocking construction blocks made entirely from commercially produced lunar-highlands regolith simulant. The team used laser powder-bed fusion to sinter the powder without an added binder, producing reconfigurable shapes a few centimeters across. The underlying University of Arkansas release was published November 10, and the study appeared in npj Advanced Manufacturing.

Why it matters

The material was LHS-1 simulant designed to resemble Apollo-era lunar samples, not dust returned from the Moon. The blocks were self-supporting, but their porosity and lower strength mean the researchers do not yet describe them as load-bearing construction. Future work must test stronger designs and determine how the printing process behaves in lunar gravity and vacuum. The result is a promising manufacturing method, not a finished Moon-base wall.
