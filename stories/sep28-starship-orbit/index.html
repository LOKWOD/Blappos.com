Starship reaches orbit for the first time, then returns three hours into a ten-hour mission

What happened

SpaceX's Starship reached orbit for the first time on September 28 and deployed 26 advanced Starlink satellites. The planned ten-hour, six-orbit flight ended after about three hours and two orbits when the company brought the vehicle home early following an engine shutdown. Starship survived re-entry, splashed down in the northern Pacific and then tipped over and caught fire.

Why it matters

The flight combined a genuine milestone with a shortened test. Reaching orbit and deploying payloads advances a launch system intended to carry larger satellite batches and support NASA's Artemis moon plans, while the early engine shutdown leaves engineers with a new reliability problem to understand. A safe return demonstrates progress; it does not turn a partial mission into a fully completed one.
