Florida Mushroom Invents the Worst Possible Combo Pack

What happened

A November 14 report highlighted a July 22 bioRxiv preprint on Galerina indica specimens collected from moss-covered palm trunks in Florida wetlands. Repeated mass-spectrometry analysis detected psilocybin and psilocin along with two amatoxin compounds, including a newly characterized compound the researchers call alpha-proamanitin. Genome sequencing found the corresponding biosynthetic genes; the authors' evolutionary analysis supports at least two independent acquisitions of psilocybin biosynthesis within Galerina.

Why it matters

The result overturns a useful-looking shortcut: blue bruising associated with psilocin chemistry does not guarantee that an unfamiliar wild mushroom lacks amatoxins. The paper is a preprint, not peer-reviewed, and it did not establish a human dose, a clinical poisoning history or the field safety of any specimen. It also did not detect alpha-amanitin itself. The defensible public-health conclusion is not to rank the mushroom's effects—it is never to eat an unidentified wild mushroom based on color, bruising or an online photo.

The Blappos angle
