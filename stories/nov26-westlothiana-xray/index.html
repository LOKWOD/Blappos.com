X-Rays Evict the ‘Oldest Reptile’ From the Reptile Family Tree

What happened

A November 26 ScienceDaily digest highlighted new high-resolution X-ray work on Westlothiana lizziae, a 345-million-year-old Scottish fossil nicknamed Lizzie and long treated as an early land-adapted amniote. The Nature study and American Museum of Natural History release report a primitive skull, an ossified internal gill skeleton and extensive tiny teeth on the palate and jaw. Those hidden features place the animal on the tetrapod stem outside the reptile, bird and mammal lineage, despite its four limbs and superficially claw-like digit tips.

Why it matters

The reclassification changes how one famous fossil is used to explain the move from water to land. Limbs, five digits and claw-like tips do not by themselves prove a fully terrestrial amniote body plan; internal anatomy can preserve the stronger ecological signal. The study does not make Lizzie a fish or show that every early tetrapod with digits lived in water. It identifies a mosaic animal with terrestrial-looking limbs and unexpectedly aquatic cranial anatomy.
