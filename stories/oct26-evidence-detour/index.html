Orlando’s $760,000 Evidence Takes a Two-Week Detour

What happened

An October 26 report followed an October 22 State Attorney release announcing that a jury found Jaythan Gilder guilty of robbery and grand theft. Prosecutors said that in February 2025 he posed as a representative negotiating for a professional basketball player, took Tiffany & Co. merchandise valued above $760,000 and used force against an employee. After his arrest, he swallowed stolen earrings; authorities monitored him for nearly two weeks before recovering them. Sentencing was set for December 7.

Why it matters

The spectacle is not the whole case. The employee’s safety, a jury verdict and a still-pending sentence are the consequential facts; the unusual recovery belongs inside that legal timeline rather than replacing it. It also demonstrates why evidence custody sometimes extends beyond an evidence room—and why a prosecution release should be identified as the prosecution’s account even after a conviction.
