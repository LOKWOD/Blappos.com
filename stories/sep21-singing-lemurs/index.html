Singing lemurs use an opera trick to carry notes through Madagascar’s forest

What happened

Researchers studying wild indris in Madagascar analyzed 83 videos of 19 animals. Reuters reported that the lemurs opened their mouths wider as notes rose, a pattern consistent with formant tuning—the same acoustic technique human sopranos use to keep high notes clear and powerful.

Why it matters

Indri songs help families stay connected and advertise territory through dense forest. Finding a comparable acoustic adjustment in another primate adds evidence about how vocal tracts shape long-distance communication. The animals are critically endangered, so the finding also brings attention to a sophisticated behavior that depends on intact habitat.

The Blappos angle

The rainforest has a resident soprano section and no microphones. Humans spent centuries inventing opera houses; indris brought the acoustics with them.
