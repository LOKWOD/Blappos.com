$30 estate-sale painting discovers it has a $250,000 résumé

What happened

UPI reported that a small Gertrude Abercrombie painting bought for $30 at a Michigan estate sale was authenticated as the long-missing 1951 work “Alderman Merriam’s Shells.” Freeman’s auction house says the 8-by-11-inch oil-on-Masonite work will be offered October 1 with a $150,000–$250,000 estimate; it has not yet sold for that amount.

Why it matters

The discovery is a reminder that an estimate is not a sale result—and that provenance and expert authentication, not a lucky price tag alone, create art-market confidence. Freeman’s says Abercrombie gave the painting to physician John Reynolds in 1952 for medical services, and it remained with his estate until the 2026 sale.
