Mars Hides Organic Carbon Under One Sheet of Dust

What happened

A ScienceDaily digest published November 12 highlighted a Science Advances study reporting macromolecular carbon in two mudstones at Bright Angel in Mars' Jezero Crater. Perseverance's SHERLOC instrument used deep-ultraviolet Raman spectroscopy to detect the material only a few micrometers below the rock surface—the shallowest such detection reported on Mars. The finding is spatially separated from earlier organic-carbon detections by Curiosity in Gale Crater.

Why it matters

Organic carbon is important raw material for habitability and biosignature research, but 'organic' describes carbon chemistry, not a confirmed biological origin. The paper says the carbon could have formed through biological or non-biological pathways and does not establish past life. Finding complex carbon in two widely separated ancient Martian environments strengthens the case that such material may be widespread and preserved, while the exact origin still requires stronger evidence—ideally laboratory analysis of returned samples.
