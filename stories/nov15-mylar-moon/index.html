The Moon Base Finds Its Insulation in an Emergency Blanket

What happened

ScienceDaily's November 15 digest describes a Penn Engineering study that compressed hundreds of thin BoPET—commonly known as Mylar—films into dense, flexible vacuum-insulation stacks. Some films carried an alumina coating. In laboratory vacuum tests the best stack reached thermal conductivity as low as 2.2 milliwatts per meter-kelvin at 0.1 megapascal while samples tolerated compressive stresses above 20 megapascals.

Why it matters

Conventional multilayer insulation works well in vacuum but is too fragile to serve as a load-bearing wall. These denser stacks suggest one material could combine very low heat transfer with substantial compression resistance, which is why the researchers discuss lunar habitats. The important limit is scale: the published work is a small laboratory model assembled by hand, not a meter-wide manufacturing process or flight-qualified Moon-base panel.
