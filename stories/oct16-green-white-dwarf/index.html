Astronomers Find the First Star Wearing Chartreuse

What happened

Reuters reported October 16 on a RAS Techniques and Instruments study identifying WD J1330+6435 as the first documented genuinely green star. The Earth-sized white dwarf lies about 280 light-years away in Draco. Rocky debris from its former planetary system polluted its dense helium atmosphere with sodium, calcium and lithium; pressure-broadened absorption by sodium removes much of the orange-to-red light, leaving a chartreuse appearance in the researchers' color reconstruction.

Why it matters

Ordinary star color mainly tracks temperature, but white-dwarf atmospheres are dense enough for trace elements to reshape broad bands of the spectrum. That makes color a clue to both extreme atomic physics and the chemistry of destroyed planetary material. The team analyzed 151 nearby white dwarfs and found an unusual-color minority, so this is a real first—not evidence that green stars are common or bright enough to look green through a backyard telescope.
