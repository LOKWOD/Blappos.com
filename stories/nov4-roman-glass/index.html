Sheffield Rebuilds Rome's 1,600-Year-Old Mood-Ring Cup

What happened

Popular Mechanics reported on November 4 that University of Sheffield technician Lisa Hollands and collaborators reproduced in quantity the dichroic glass effect made famous by the fourth-century Lycurgus Cup. The recreated material appears jade green in reflected light and ruby red when light passes through it. Using a recipe derived from an original glass fragment, the team varied starting materials, furnace conditions and oxygen levels until tiny gold-and-silver-bearing structures produced the desired optical behavior.

Why it matters

The project reproduces the material's color-changing behavior, not a complete record of how one Roman workshop made the surviving cup. The original object remains a British Museum artifact; the new material was used by glass artist Elliot Walker in a contemporary sculpture scheduled to debut November 6. The work also informs modern photonic glass and research on immobilizing nuclear waste, where controlling crystals and particles inside glass matters.
