Texas Knits a Sock That Makes Walking 2% Less Expensive

What happened

ScienceAlert reported on November 16 that a prototype sock made walking slightly less metabolically costly in a small treadmill study. Twelve healthy young adults completed five-minute walking trials while the custom garment squeezed and stiffened the forefoot. Compared with ordinary socks, the prototype increased longitudinal foot stiffness by about 65% and reduced whole-body metabolic rate by about 2%; the researchers also measured lower calf-muscle fascicle shortening velocities.

Why it matters

Two percent is a real laboratory result and a very small proof of concept, not a consumer-performance promise. The experiment involved 12 healthy participants, short treadmill trials and a highly specific prototype; it did not test long hikes, running, older adults, disabled users, injury prevention or everyday comfort. The authors report a related patent application, which belongs beside the result. The useful finding is narrower: changing foot stiffness from outside the body may alter walking energetics enough to measure.
