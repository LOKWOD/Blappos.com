North Carolina Blueberries Have a Five-Inch Basement Tenant

What happened

A North Carolina State University research digest published November 6 identified the tile-horned prionus, Prionus imbricornis, as the long-mysterious larva damaging blueberry roots in North Carolina. The team trapped adults at six farms in four counties and compared standardized DNA barcode segments from larvae with known adult sequences, obtaining 98–99% matches. The larvae live underground, can grow to about five inches and may consume enough root tissue to kill a bush.

Why it matters

The species-level identification matters because similar-looking longhorn beetle larvae can attack different parts of a plant and require different management. NC State says guessing the wrong species can lead to an ineffective treatment and harm non-target insects. North Carolina is the first state to document this species feeding on blueberries, and the university says no insecticides are currently labeled for it in that crop; trials are underway, so the result is a diagnostic starting point rather than a ready-made control recommendation.
