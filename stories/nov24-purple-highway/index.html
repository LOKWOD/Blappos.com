Two States Follow a Purple Highway Stripe Back to One Leaking Truck

What happened

News 12 reported on November 24 that the purple streak seen across highways in New Jersey and New York came from a leaking tractor-trailer operated by XPO. New York's Department of Environmental Conservation described the material as a water-soluble organic dye used in car washes. New Jersey officials identified the principal substance as sorbitan monopalmitate, also called Span 40. Somerset County's hazardous-materials team tested the spill and found it nonhazardous.

Why it matters

The striking color turned an ordinary freight leak into a two-state public mystery, but color alone does not establish toxicity. The agency and county assessments support the nonhazardous conclusion while leaving practical questions—how far it spread, how quickly it will wash away and how the container failed—outside the published evidence. The named chemical is used as an emulsifier in multiple products; that does not mean the road spill was food or cosmetic material.
