Swiss glaciers lose more than 5% of their ice in one year

What happened

Switzerland's glaciers lost more than 5% of their total ice volume during the first eight months of 2026, according to the Swiss glacier-monitoring network cited by Reuters and UPI on October 1. Researchers linked the near-record annual loss to a dry winter, limited snow cover and intense summer heat. The total decline over the past five years is now about one-fifth.

Why it matters

One unusually bad melt season matters because thinner glaciers expose darker ground, reduce their ability to reflect heat and leave less ice to survive the next hot year. The monitoring result is a national aggregate, not a claim that every Swiss glacier lost the same share. The figure also describes physical loss already measured in 2026, not a forecast or a symbolic climate target.
