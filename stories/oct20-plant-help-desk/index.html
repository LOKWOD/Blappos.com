A Startup Gives 125 Plant Species a Help Desk

What happened

TechCrunch reported on October 20 that Electric Plant has built sensors and an AI model called Bombadil to interpret electrical signals from living plants. The company says its Bud sensor passes electrodes through the cambium and streams readings to the cloud, where the model can classify patterns associated with hydration, growth and stress across more than 125 species. Those performance and water-saving figures are company claims, not an independent agronomic trial.

Why it matters

Plant electrophysiology is real: changes in a plant's electrical activity can carry information about its condition. A system that spots stress before visible symptoms could help growers respond earlier and use water more precisely. The evidence clock matters, though. Electric Plant's FAQ describes more than 125 species, while its preprint describes a pretraining corpus covering 100 species; those are not necessarily the same measurement, and neither number alone proves the model will generalize to every crop, field or climate.
