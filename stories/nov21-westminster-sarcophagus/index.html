Westminster's Roman Coffin Is Probably Hosting the Wrong Millennium

What happened

ScienceDaily's November 21 research digest revisited a limestone sarcophagus excavated at Westminster Abbey in 1869. Its inscription names the Roman-era Valerius Amandinus, but a medieval cross was later cut into the lid. Radiocarbon dating places the skeleton inside at AD 987–1052, and bone analysis describes a man roughly 35–49 years old and about 5 feet 9 inches tall. Archaeologist Warwick Rodwell argues that the occupant was probably a high-ranking medieval monk; the leading candidates are Abbots Aelfwig, who died around 1020, and Wulnoth, who died in 1049.

Why it matters

The coffin, the skeleton and the name carved on the stone belong to different evidentiary clocks. The Roman inscription identifies the sarcophagus's first known occupant, while the date range and re-carved lid show that the container was reused centuries later. Status, location and chronology support an abbot, but they do not choose conclusively between the two candidates. Calling the mystery fully solved would outrun the paper and the Abbey's own account.
