Dwarf Snakes Discover Four-Wheel Drive Without Wheels

What happened

ScienceDaily reported on October 24 that Toho University researchers had documented a rolling locomotion mode in dwarf reedsnakes using synchronized multi-angle video. In controlled trials, the snakes reached peak rolling speeds of 1.72 to 3.69 meters per second—about four times their lateral-undulation speed—and followed a roughly 20-centimeter-wide track. Their heads stayed forward and relatively stable while the body rolled, and the team published the work in Current Biology.

Why it matters

A rolling vertebrate is more than a novelty reel. The measurements distinguish sustained, directed locomotion from a defensive tumble or accidental fall: the animals repeatedly combined rolling and ordinary strokes while keeping the head oriented for navigation. That expands the known movement repertoire of limbless animals and gives biomechanics and robotics researchers a compact example of how body geometry can trade continuous contact for short bursts of speed.
