An Old Elevator Texts ‘Gravity’ Through a Brick Wall at One Bit a Minute

What happened

A November 24 ScienceDaily digest highlighted a University of Queensland experiment in which researchers used an old elevator and its 1.8-metric-ton counterweight to encode binary signals in tiny changes to the local gravitational field. A sensitive gravimeter in an adjacent bathroom detected the changes through a brick wall. The team transmitted the word ‘Gravity’ at roughly one bit per minute, so the one-word message took about an hour.

Why it matters

Gravity is difficult to block, but it is also extraordinarily weak at laboratory scales. The experiment is a proof of principle that a controlled moving mass can carry information where ordinary electromagnetic signals may be blocked or monitored. It is not a practical replacement for radio: the apparatus was building-sized, the data rate was extremely slow and the researchers say substantial engineering would be needed for real-world use.
