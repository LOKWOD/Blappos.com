Voyager 1 Reaches the Address Where Hello Takes Two Days

What happened

NASA calculated that Voyager 1 reached one light-day from Earth at 5:16:07 a.m. EST on November 18—about 16.094 billion miles, or 25.902 billion kilometers, away. A radio command now needs about 24 hours to reach the probe, and its answer needs another 24 hours to return. The milestone arrived 49 years, two months and 11 days after launch. Separately, engineers said Voyager 1 had been transmitting only a carrier signal since November 14, with no science or engineering data in it, even though the carrier indicates the antenna remains pointed toward Earth and the transmitter is operating.

Why it matters

One light-day is a communications threshold, not a wall in space: nothing physically changed when Voyager crossed it. The practical change has accumulated for decades—every diagnostic round trip now consumes roughly two days before engineers can even evaluate the response. NASA says the current carrier-only problem is probably in data formatting or an onboard computer, but that diagnosis could take weeks or longer. The carrier and trajectory models can confirm the distance milestone; neither proves that the missing telemetry issue is solved.
