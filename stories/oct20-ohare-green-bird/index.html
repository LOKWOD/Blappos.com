O'Hare's Green Bird Chooses Fight, Not Flight

What happened

UPI reported on October 20 that video from Chicago O'Hare's Terminal 5 showed a large green bird swooping near travelers, landing on people and luggage and trying to bite. The encounter happened on October 13, so the incident clock and publication clock are different. A worker eventually caught the bird with a cloth and it was taken for veterinary care. Reports called it a parrot, but no source available at publication supplied a confirmed species identification.

Why it matters

An animal loose inside a crowded terminal creates risk for the animal and for travelers who may react unpredictably. The humane resolution came from containing it rather than escalating the commotion. Keeping the species uncertain is also basic sourcing discipline: a vivid color, a hooked bill and an eyewitness label can support a useful description without becoming a taxonomic identification.

The Blappos angle

Verified video reporting, the two clocks and the species boundary end above. The Blappos interpretation: Terminal 5 has added a very personal bag check, administered by a passenger with no boarding group.
