A Mushroom Apparently Hires Hundreds of Tiny People

What happened

ScienceDaily's October 25 research digest revisited a 2026 Mycologia study in which DNA sequencing linked reports from southwest China and the northern Philippines to Lanmaoa asiatica. Independent accounts describe lilliputian hallucinations: numerous tiny human, animal or fantasy figures moving through the real environment. The University of Utah team found no close genomic matches to known biosynthetic genes for psilocybin or ibotenic acid, so the responsible compound and mechanism remain unknown.

Why it matters

The result is an identification and a research lead, not an invitation to experiment. Similar reports across separated cultures make the phenomenon scientifically interesting, while the missing chemical explanation is the important uncertainty—not a license to fill the gap with folklore. Wild mushrooms are frequently misidentified, potentially poisonous and unsuitable for self-testing; the public value is in documented specimens, sequencing and controlled chemistry.
