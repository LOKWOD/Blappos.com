WILDLIFE SIGHTING · CRYSTAL COVE, CALIFORNIA

A probable yellow-bellied sea snake—with uncertainty left intact

Reported claim: Video from Crystal Cove appeared to show a yellow-bellied sea snake. Marine naturalist Chad Steffen said its markings and body shape fit the species.

Evidence: UPI’s September 17 report documents the video and attributed identification. The Australian Museum species profile supplies independent background on range, habitat, venom and behavior.

Uncertainty: UPI said officials had not formally confirmed the species. Blappos therefore calls it “appeared to show” and “reported sighting,” not a settled agency identification. The safe action for an unfamiliar stranded snake remains distance and notification of park staff.

REUSABLE REFERENCE

The five-step strange-news verification checklist

  1. Date the claim: record when the event happened and when the reporting was published; a resurfaced clip is not a new event.
  2. Find the closest source: prefer the responsible agency, record authority, institution, named witness or original research over an unattributed repost.
  3. Preserve attribution: distinguish what a source proves from what a witness, company or expert says. Do not silently upgrade “appears to be” into “is.”
  4. Check the consequence: verify the number, location, safety implication and outcome that make the story matter—not merely its most shareable detail.
  5. Write the boundary: state the unresolved point, identify the illustration as artwork and place satire after the verified facts.

Wildlife evidence ladder: agency confirmation outranks a qualified identification, which outranks a witness description, which outranks an unattributed social post. When the strongest available evidence sits below formal confirmation, the headline and copy must say so.

PUBLIC LANDS · YELLOWSTONE NATIONAL PARK

20,100 trash pieces, 5,600 rocks and sticks, and more than 280 hats

Verified claim: By early September 2026, trained Yellowstone geology staff had removed those record-setting totals from hydrothermal areas, along with items including AirPods, retainers, a rubber duck, an Uno card and glittery pink slime.

Primary evidence: Margery Price of the Yellowstone National Park Geology Program wrote the September 14 USGS/Yellowstone Volcano Observatory inventory. UPI’s September 16 report brought the inventory into the day’s news cycle.

Why the primary source matters: USGS explains that foreign material can clog water supplies, divert runoff or introduce pollution, and says visitors should secure loose belongings, use trash cans and remain on paths and boardwalks.
