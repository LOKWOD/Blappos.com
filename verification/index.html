RECORDS · BROOKFIELD, WISCONSIN

754 matched salt-and-pepper packet pairs

Verified claim: Linda Schulz, 67, reclaimed the Guinness World Records title with 754 matching pairs after first holding it with 494 and losing it to a 594-pair collection.

Evidence: Ben Hooper’s September 16 UPI report, which attributes the record and direct quotation to Guinness World Records.

Boundary: The collection totals, title history, first Arby’s pair and Hawaiian Airlines favorites are reported facts. “Strategic seasoning reserve” is satire.

PUBLIC LANDS · YELLOWSTONE NATIONAL PARK

20,100 trash pieces, 5,600 rocks and sticks, and more than 280 hats

Verified claim: By early September 2026, trained Yellowstone geology staff had removed those record-setting totals from hydrothermal areas, along with items including AirPods, retainers, a rubber duck, an Uno card and glittery pink slime.

Primary evidence: Margery Price of the Yellowstone National Park Geology Program wrote the September 14 USGS/Yellowstone Volcano Observatory inventory. UPI’s September 16 report brought the inventory into the day’s news cycle.

Why the primary source matters: USGS explains that foreign material can clog water supplies, divert runoff or introduce pollution, and says visitors should secure loose belongings, use trash cans and remain on paths and boardwalks. The illustrated retrieval scene is representative artwork, not a photograph of one cleanup.

MISINFORMATION · JACKSON, NEW JERSEY

A viral tiger, numerous 911 calls and no loose animal

Verified claim: Jackson Township police said there was no tiger loose near Six Flags Great Adventure, the park said every animal was accounted for, and police asked people not to tie up emergency lines over the circulating video.

Evidence: UPI’s September 16 report reproduces the police confirmation and attributes the animal count to Six Flags. The tiger shown on the Blappos card’s phone screen is an illustration of an unverified viral claim, not a frame presented as evidence.

Boundary: The calls and official denial are facts. A tiger “living inside the engagement metrics” is commentary.

FIELD OBSERVATION · UNITED STATES

One hamburger’s pickle-versus-patty comparison

Verified claim: McDonald’s official hamburger page identifies a beef patty, pickle, onions, ketchup and mustard as standard components.

Limit: The thickness comparison was an observation about the single photographed sandwich that inspired the card. It is not presented as a chain-wide measurement, product recall, laboratory result or claim that every patty is thinner than every pickle.

Boundary: The ingredient list is a product fact; the “beef privacy screen” is the joke.
