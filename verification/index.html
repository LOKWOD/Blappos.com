ART MARKET · MICHIGAN

A $30 purchase with a $150,000–$250,000 auction estimate

Verified claim: The work is Gertrude Abercrombie’s 1951 oil-on-Masonite painting Alderman Merriam’s Shells, bought for $30 at a Michigan estate sale and authenticated before its October 1 auction. The painting measures 8 by 11 inches.

Primary evidence: Freeman’s official lot record gives the title, medium, dimensions, estimate and provenance. The auction house’s press note describes the 2026 estate-sale discovery and authentication; UPI’s September 18 report establishes the day’s news item.

Calculation and limit: $150,000 ÷ $30 = 5,000; $250,000 ÷ $30 ≈ 8,333. Those are estimate-to-purchase-price multiples, not profit, return or a completed sale. Buyer’s premium, taxes, ownership costs and the auction result are not included.

Boundary: The purchase, authentication, provenance and estimate are sourced facts. The bread-maker comparison is satire, and the card’s shell painting is original illustration—not a reproduction of Abercrombie’s work.

CRITICAL INFRASTRUCTURE · UNITED KINGDOM

A one-millisecond trigger with a multi-day consequence

Verified claim: NATS said a paused aircraft-code request resumed incorrectly after a higher-priority task, corrupting output and subsequent data. The triggering sequence occurred within a millisecond; national restrictions remained for six hours while the system restarted.

Primary evidence: NATS’ September 18 preliminary report explains the defect, operational restrictions, safety status, mitigation and planned fix. Associated Press reported more than 2,000 cancellations and disruption lasting two days.

Interpretation guardrail: “One millisecond” describes the timing of the software interaction—not the outage duration and not a cancellation rate. “More than 2,000 flights” is the aggregate reported consequence. Dividing the two would manufacture a meaningless statistic.

Boundary: NATS said safety was never in question. The departure-board group therapy is satire; the illustrated control room is not documentary evidence or a reconstruction of the actual interface.

WILDLIFE · RIO VISTA, CALIFORNIA

A cataloged humpback approximately 60 miles inland

Verified claim: An adult humpback traveled through San Francisco Bay into the Sacramento–San Joaquin Delta and was seen near Rio Vista, about 60 miles from the Pacific. The Marine Mammal Center matched its fluke to an animal cataloged for 24 years with 15 prior sightings.

Evidence: Associated Press’ September 18 report attributes the identification, distance and public guidance to the Marine Mammal Center. The center’s reporting guidance is the direct route for marine-mammal sightings and distress reports.

Uncertainty: The reason for the animal’s route was unknown. Witness video suggested it was swimming normally, but that does not establish health, destination or a need for rescue. The responsible action is distance, no course alteration and reporting—not pursuit.

Boundary: The location, catalog history and distance guidance are reported facts. “Touring Sacramento” is the joke, and the card is an illustration rather than a photograph of the sighting.

REUSABLE REFERENCE

The five-step strange-news verification checklist

  1. Date the claim: record when the event happened and when the reporting was published; a resurfaced clip is not a new event.
  2. Find the closest source: prefer the responsible agency, record authority, institution, named witness or original research over an unattributed repost.
  3. Preserve attribution: distinguish what a source proves from what a witness, company or expert says. Do not silently upgrade “appears to be” into “is.”
  4. Check the consequence: verify the number, location, safety implication and outcome that make the story matter—not merely its most shareable detail.
  5. Write the boundary: state the unresolved point, identify the illustration as artwork and place satire after the verified facts.

Wildlife evidence ladder: agency confirmation outranks a qualified identification, which outranks a witness description, which outranks an unattributed social post. When the strongest available evidence sits below formal confirmation, the headline and copy must say so.
